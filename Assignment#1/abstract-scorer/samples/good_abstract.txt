Large language models (LLMs) have demonstrated remarkable capabilities in natural language understanding, yet their susceptibility to prompt injection attacks remains a critical security concern for deployed applications. In this paper, we present InjectGuard, a lightweight detection framework that identifies malicious instructions embedded in user-supplied content before they reach the model. Our approach combines a fine-tuned classifier trained on 42,000 labeled prompts with a rule-based canary mechanism that flags attempts to override system instructions. We evaluate InjectGuard on three public benchmarks and a newly collected dataset of 3,100 real-world attacks harvested from production logs. InjectGuard achieves a detection F1 score of 0.94, outperforming the strongest prior baseline by 11 points, while adding less than 8 ms of latency per request. We further show that the framework generalizes to unseen attack families with only a 3% drop in recall. Our code and dataset are publicly released to support future research on LLM security.
